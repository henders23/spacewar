CORE RULES · DRAFT v0.2 · WORKING TITLE

SEALED ORDERS

A game of capital ships slugging it out in open space. Both admirals write orders in secret, every ship moves at the same moment, and then every gun fires at the same moment. Fistfuls of d6 decide who is still flying.

2 players or vs AI d6 only Free movement, no squares 20–40 min per duel

1 · The turn

Every turn has four phases. Nobody goes first: both sides commit, then the game resolves everything together. Distances are in units (u). The battle zone is 120u × 80u, and a battleship is about 7u long.

PHASE 1

Orders

In secret, each player places a destination marker for every ship and may give it one special order. Orders are sealed once committed.

PHASE 2

Movement

Orders are revealed and all ships move at once, each sweeping a smooth curve to its marker. Then check for collisions and ships leaving the zone.

PHASE 3

Gunnery

Every weapon that bears fires. Each player rolls their own dice. Damage is applied only after all ships have fired, so a ship that is destroyed this phase still gets its final broadside off.

PHASE 4

End phase

Fires burn, damage control crews try to repair criticals, and destroyed ships are removed. Check for victory.

2 · Orders & movement

Every ship has a Speed and a Turn value. Its marker must sit inside its manoeuvre zone:

  • between half Speed and full Speed away, measured straight from the ship's centre (capital ships cannot stop), and
  • no more than Turn degrees either side of the bow.

The ship sweeps a constant curve that starts along its current heading and ends on the marker. It finishes pointing along that curve, so its heading changes by twice the marker's bearing. A marker 30° off the bow means a 60° turn. That is the trade-off: hard turns spend distance and leave you predictable.

Ships pass over and under each other, so paths never block. Only the end positions matter for collisions.

3 · Firing arcs & gunnery

Players roll their own dice: the firer rolls to hit and on the critical table, and the defender rolls armour saves. In the end phase each player rolls damage control for their own ship.

Find which of the firer's arcs the target's centre sits in, and measure the range between centres. Every weapon covering that arc and in range rolls its dice. Each die that meets the to-hit number scores a hit.

RANGETO HIT

  • Narrow profile, −1 to hit: the firer is in the target's fore or aft arc, so the target is end-on.
  • All Ahead Full, −1 · Lock On, +1 · Bridge Hit, −1
  • A natural 6 always hits and a natural 1 always misses. To-hit numbers never go below 2+ or above 6+.

4 · Shields, armour & damage

Shields by facing

Each ship has four shield values: fore, port, starboard and aft. Use the facing the shots come in on, which is whichever of the target's arcs the firer sits in. That shield cancels that many hits outright. The defender removes ordinary hits first and 6s last, so lucky shots tend to punch through. Swing your strong side toward the enemy.

Armour saves

Roll a d6 for every hit that gets past the shields. Meeting the Armour value saves it (a 1 always fails). Every failed save is 1 point of hull damage.

Lances

Lance hits ignore shields completely, and each failed save does 2 damage. They are short-ranged and only fire through the bow.

Crippled

At half Hull or less a ship is crippled: each broadside rolls half its dice (round up). At 0 Hull the ship is destroyed.

5 · Critical hits

Any to-hit roll of a natural 6 that ends up causing damage (it got past both shields and armour) also triggers a critical. Roll a d6 on the table. Criticals stack, and they take effect after gunnery.

6 · Collisions, disengaging & victory

Collision

If two ships finish movement with their centres within 4u, each takes D3 damage with no saves, and they are pushed apart. A ship with a Ram Prow that has the enemy in its fore arc deals D6 instead.

Leaving the zone

A ship that ends movement outside the 120u × 80u battle zone has disengaged. It is out of the battle and counts as lost.

Victory

Destroy or drive off the enemy flagship. If both ships go down in the same turn, which simultaneous fire makes possible, the result is mutual destruction.

Designer's notes · for the online build

  • Server-authoritative turns. Clients send only {destination, special order} per ship. The server checks each against the manoeuvre zone and waits for both players (or a turn timer). In gunnery it then asks each player in turn for their roll, which the server generates on the player's click so nobody can fake a die, and streams the results to both clients.
  • Balance snapshot. In 1,500 AI-vs-AI duels using these profiles (with facing shields), Resolute won about 51%, Maw of Ruin 32%, and 17% ended in mutual destruction. A typical duel runs about 6–7 turns. Treat this as a starting point; the AI is a simple one-turn look-ahead.
  • Open questions. Should players pick target priority once fleets grow beyond 1v1? Should destroyed ships leave wreck hazards? Should firing happen mid-move (in tick slices) rather than at end of move? Are there altitude bands for 3D depth?
